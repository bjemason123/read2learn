# Competitive and adjacent landscape: tools for defining and managing a personal (self-directed) learning curriculum

Research date: 2026-11-07. Method: about 24 searches and fetches. Primary pages were fetched where the fetch succeeded (Readwise pricing, Google blog, roadmap.sh homepage, TechCrunch, Chalmers study record). Several pages returned 403/404, and many "reviews" are aggregator or vendor blogs, flagged below. Anything I could not verify is under Gaps. Prices are 2026 snapshots and the sources sometimes disagree.

## Which tools exist for defining a personal curriculum or learning path, by category?

### Takeaway
The market splits into three layers that rarely meet. Roadmap and course-path products give structure but not personalisation. Reading and knowledge tools give capture, notes and review but no curriculum. AI tutors give personalised, Socratic explanation but little persistent, user-owned plan structure. I found no verified product that combines a goal, user-attached reading material and a living plan in one place.

### Cited Findings

#### (a) Roadmap and curriculum builders
- roadmap.sh homepage lists 97 roadmaps (31 role-based, 61 skill-based, 5 best-practice). It also lists an "AI Tutor" nav item, lesson packs, video courses, guides, a Discord of about 52K members, about 371K GitHub stars and over 3.2M registered users. The fetched homepage did not mention custom roadmaps, progress tracking or pricing. — [roadmap.sh](https://roadmap.sh/) (the /pricing URL returned 404)
- Metacademy is built on a prerequisite graph of concepts. Each concept carries a short description, learning goals, a rough time estimate and pointers to learning resources, and the graph is used to generate a learning plan for a target concept. Content is centred on machine learning and probabilistic AI. — [hunch.net, "Metacademy: a package manager for knowledge"](https://hunch.net/?p=2714); [Stephen Downes summary](https://downes.ca/post/61814)
- Metacademy "roadmaps" are separate, human-written overview pages. User-created roadmaps were a planned feature in the project wiki. I did not confirm whether it shipped, and all the sources are 2014-era. — [Metacademy content wiki: Roadmaps](https://github-wiki-see.page/m/metacademy/metacademy-content/wiki/Roadmaps)
- LinkedIn Learning "learning paths" are curated playlists of courses on a topic or career track. Individual subscribers can reach more than 70 paths. Finishing a path or course can earn a certificate, and some paths include quizzes, articles and project templates. — [LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/learning/answer/a703777); [University of Arkansas KB](https://uark.teamdynamix.com/TDClient/33/Portal/KB/ArticleDet?ID=1450)
- A third-party 2025 comparison lists LinkedIn Learning at $29.99/month or $239.88/year. This is unverified against LinkedIn's own pricing page. — [Jobright.ai comparison](https://jobright.ai/blog/coursera-vs-linkedin-learning-pros-and-cons/)
- Coursera offers Specializations and learning paths (multi-course sequences). Coursera Coach is a generative-AI tutor grounded in Coursera course content, with lecture summaries, guided practice with spaced repetition, and pre-assessment review. That description dates from 2023-24 announcements, so I do not know its current scope. — [Stanford Cardinal at Work](https://cardinalatwork.stanford.edu/careers/professional-development/free-learning-platforms); [edScoop](https://edscoop.com/coursera-ai-powered-online-learning-coach/); [UMich Record](https://record.umich.edu/articles/ai-powered-coursera-coach-aids-interactive-instruction)
- Khan Academy core course content is free, and Khanmigo (AI tutor) is listed at $4/month or $44/year for individuals. Khanmigo is described as available to learners of all ages in the US. One review says mastery tracking is in the free tier. Prices come from aggregator sites. — [Khanmigo pricing page](https://khanmigo.ai/pricing) (only partly captured); [Khanmigo for learners](https://khanmigo.ai/learners); [Khan Academy support: Adult Learner](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/ka/community/posts/33227224735501-Adult-Learner)
- Notion and Obsidian "learning tracker" templates are sold in volume (Gumroad etc.). They bundle goals, schedules and progress databases. One DEV post criticises most Notion learning trackers as "glorified course lists" lacking spaced repetition and active recall. — [DEV: Learning Tracker in Notion](https://dev.to/lux_seminare/how-to-build-learning-tracker-in-notion-3148); [Learning OS template](https://antonydiaz.me/templates/learning-os); [Gumroad learning journey tracker](https://grizzlytemplates.gumroad.com/l/learningjourneytrackernotiontemplate)
- Users also combine Zotero with Notion (a Zotero collection syncs into a Notion literature database), or Obsidian with Zotero plus Kanban or Projects plugins, to build reading queues. This is a DIY assembly of several tools. — [Medium: PhD Tips](https://medium.com/@GenShanahan/phd-tips-the-project-and-knowledge-management-template-a7f67e94d9ae); [DEV: Readwise vs Notion vs Obsidian](https://dev.to/airabbit/readwise-vs-notion-vs-obsidian-the-ultimate-knowledge-management-showdown-2be5)

#### (b) Reading and knowledge tools
- Readwise (official pricing page): Full (Reader + Highlights) $9.99/mo billed yearly; Highlights only $7.99; Highlights Lite $5.59; Reader only $7.99. A 30-day free trial, students 50% off, no permanent free tier. Full includes unlimited saving of articles, PDFs, EPUBs, emails, videos and RSS, Daily Review, highlight search and imports. Highlights plans include flashcards and exports to Notion and Obsidian. Reader includes Ghostreader AI. — [Readwise pricing](https://readwise.io/pricing)
- Readwise Reader user complaints (secondary sources): cost versus value, no free tier, opaque billing after trial, weak offline reliability, poor search. These are aggregator and review-blog claims. The "no Reader-only plan" claim in one June 2026 source conflicts with the official page listing a Reader-only plan. — [Vendr](https://vendr.com/blog/do-you-use-readwise-app-how-much-are-you-paying-for-the-service); [Asian Efficiency review](https://www.asianefficiency.com/productivity/readwise-reader-review/); [aitoolsatlas](https://aitoolsatlas.ai/tools/readwise/pros-cons)
- StoryGraph supports reading goals by books, pages or hours, multiple concurrent goals and community challenges. I found no official confirmation of per-book notes. Goodreads is criticised for dated design, bugs and unresponsiveness. Reviewers report that migrating from Goodreads to StoryGraph or Hardcover loses personal notes, reading dates and editions. Anecdotal. — [Reedsy comparison](https://Reedsy.com/discovery/blog/goodreads-vs-storygraph); [unstar.app comparison](https://unstar.app/fr/blog/goodreads-storygraph-fable-hardcover-bookly-reading-tracker-apps-ranked-2026); [PopSci on StoryGraph](https://www.popsci.com/diy/storygraph/)
- RemNote is a notes app with built-in spaced repetition. 2026 aggregators say it is free with limits, Pro about $8/month annual, with a lifetime tier of about $96. Anki desktop and Android are free, and iOS is a one-off paid app (sources disagree on price: $25 vs an implausible $24.99/month). Mochi pricing was not found. All sources are aggregators or vendor blogs. — [RemNote blog: Anki alternatives](https://www.remnote.com/blog/best-anki-alternatives); [toolradar Anki](https://toolradar.com/tools/anki/pricing); [learn-log comparison](https://learn-log.pages.dev/vs/anki-vs-remnote)

#### (c) AI-driven tutors and planners
- Gemini Guided Learning launched 6 Aug 2025 in the Gemini app, built on LearnLM in Gemini 2.5. It asks probing questions, breaks problems into steps, and gives multimodal responses with images, diagrams, videos and interactive quizzes. Educators can share a Guided Learning link in Google Classroom. The Google blog does not mention learning plans or progress tracking. — [Google blog](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/education/guided-learning/); [TechCrunch, 6 Aug 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/06/google-takes-on-chatgpts-study-mode-with-new-guided-learning-tool-in-gemini/)
- Gemini can also generate flashcards and study guides from quiz results or class materials. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/06/google-takes-on-chatgpts-study-mode-with-new-guided-learning-tool-in-gemini/)
- Learn Your Way (Google Research / Labs, 16 Sep 2025) turns an uploaded textbook or PDF into personalised study material. Learners pick a grade level and interests, and it generates rewritten text, narrated slides, audio dialogues, section quizzes and mind maps. It is a research experiment with a waitlist for user-uploaded material. Its trial had 60 students aged 15-18 on one chapter (about 9% better immediately, about 11% better 3-5 days later per Google summaries; outlets report slightly different figures). Researchers say it is unclear which features drove the gains. — [Google blog: Learn Your Way](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/education/learn-your-way/); [Maginative](https://www.maginative.com/article/learn-your-way-googles-new-ai-transforms-static-textbooks-into-interactive-personalized-lessons/)
- ChatGPT Study Mode launched July 2025 (a week or so before Guided Learning), free on all plans, with guided questioning instead of direct answers. Some coverage says it is system-prompt based and uses ChatGPT memory. OpenAI's own page returned 403, so this rests on press. — [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/openai-launches-study-mode-for-chatgpt-while-education-users-are-told-to-wait-and-learn-later/); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/ai/chatgpts-study-mode-will-guide-students-to-an-answer-stey-by-step-180614172.html); [Glasp comparison](https://glasp.ai/articles/ai-study-modes-compared)
- Claude Learning Mode was introduced April 2025 as part of Claude for Education and later extended to regular Claude.ai users via the styles menu (plus two learning variants in Claude Code). It asks follow-up questions and encourages reflection. — [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/ai/claudes-new-learning-modes-take-on-chatgpts-study-mode-heres-what-they-do) (page body not retrievable; details from search snippets and [echai.ventures overview](https://echai.ventures/how-founders-use-ai/learning-with-ai/which-ai-assistants-have-a-study-or-learning-mode-and-how-do-i-turn-it-on))
- NotebookLM: Google's blog dates flashcards, quizzes and a "Learning Guide" tutor mode to September 2025. Progress on flashcards and quizzes is saved across sessions, and there are Video Overviews. Plan limits and 2026 specifics come from low-authority blogs and are unconfirmed. — [Google blog: 6 ways to use NotebookLM](https://blog.google/technology/google-labs/notebooklm-student-features/); [Chrome Unboxed](https://chromeunboxed.com/massive-notebooklm-update-adds-ai-powered-flashcards-quizzes-and-a-personal-tutor-mode/)
- AI "curriculum generators" found are mostly aimed at teachers or corporate L&D: Coursebox (course outline from a topic or documents, from €25/month), Disco (builder from $359/month, per a Disco blog), MagicSchool, Diffit and Curipod. Prep Edu (language and test-prep) generates study plans by level and goal. These are vendor-authored roundups. — [Disco roundup](https://www.disco.co/blog/best-ai-curriculum-generator-internal-ld-2026); [Coursebox](https://www.coursebox.ai/); [Forasoft comparison](https://www.forasoft.com/blog/article/automated-lesson-plan-generation-software); [Prep Edu (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prep_Edu)

### Inferences
- The generative tutors (Study Mode, Guided Learning, Learning Mode) are session-scoped Socratic modes. The sources reviewed do not describe them as maintaining a user-owned multi-week plan with prerequisites and milestones. Absence of mention is not proof, so verify in-product.
- Roadmap products (roadmap.sh, Metacademy, LinkedIn paths) are one-size-fits-all or expert-authored. They do not start from a learner's own goal or from reading they already hold.
- I found no verified direct competitor for "goal, attached reading, living plan, notes, tags, concept graph". The nearest DIY substitutes are Notion/Obsidian templates plus Readwise or Zotero.

### Gaps
- Not verified at all: Duolingo adaptive path (no source fetched), Zotero official features, Obsidian official features and plugins, Notion official features, Mochi, Class Central My Courses and edX learning paths, Coursera Plus path pricing, Goodreads official features.
- roadmap.sh custom roadmaps, progress tracking and AI roadmap generator: the homepage fetch did not mention them and the pricing URL returned 404. I have not confirmed them from a fetched source.
- Metacademy's current operational status (the sources are 2014-era).
- 2024-2026 AI "personalised curriculum generator" startups for individual adults: the search returned only teacher and L&D tools. I did not find a verified consumer product. This may reflect search limits, not true absence, so a targeted Product Hunt search is worth doing.
- Official OpenAI and Anthropic pages were not retrievable, so launch details rest on press.

## For each notable tool: how goals are defined, sequencing and prerequisites, progress tracking, retrieval practice, pricing and complaints

### Takeaway
Goal definition is mostly implicit (you pick a pre-made path or a topic). Prerequisite graphs exist only in expert-authored systems (Metacademy). Retrieval practice is strong in flashcard tools (Anki, RemNote, Readwise Highlights) and appearing in AI tutors, but it is bolted on rather than attached to a goal-scoped reading plan.

### Cited Findings
- Goal and sequencing, Metacademy: you choose a target concept and the prerequisite graph yields an ordered plan, with time estimates and resource pointers per concept. This is the clearest prerequisite model found. — [hunch.net](https://hunch.net/?p=2714)
- Goal and sequencing, roadmap.sh and LinkedIn: the learner selects from fixed role, skill or career paths, with no user-defined goal. — [roadmap.sh](https://roadmap.sh/); [LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/learning/answer/a703777)
- Retrieval practice, Readwise: Daily Review resurfaces highlights, and Highlights plans include flashcards. — [Readwise pricing](https://readwise.io/pricing)
- Retrieval practice, Coursera Coach: described as having guided practice with spaced repetition. — [edScoop](https://edscoop.com/coursera-ai-powered-online-learning-coach/)
- Retrieval practice, Gemini and NotebookLM: interactive quizzes, flashcards and study guides are generated on request. NotebookLM saves flashcard and quiz progress across sessions. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/06/google-takes-on-chatgpts-study-mode-with-new-guided-learning-tool-in-gemini/); [Chrome Unboxed](https://chromeunboxed.com/massive-notebooklm-update-adds-ai-powered-flashcards-quizzes-and-a-personal-tutor-mode/)
- Progress tracking, StoryGraph: reading goals by books, pages or hours and challenges. This is volume-based, not mastery-based. — [Reedsy comparison](https://Reedsy.com/discovery/blog/goodreads-vs-storygraph)
- Pricing snapshot: Readwise $9.99/mo (Full, annual); Khanmigo $4/mo or $44/yr; RemNote about $8/mo (annual); LinkedIn Learning about $19.99-29.99/mo (third-party); ChatGPT Study Mode free on all plans; Gemini's student offer moved to $9.99/month after the free 12-month offer expired 11 Mar 2026 (per one 2026 comparison). — [Readwise](https://readwise.io/pricing); [Khanmigo](https://khanmigo.ai/pricing); [RemNote blog](https://www.remnote.com/blog/best-anki-alternatives); [Jobright](https://jobright.ai/blog/coursera-vs-linkedin-learning-pros-and-cons/); [echai.ventures](https://echai.ventures/how-founders-use-ai/learning-with-ai/which-ai-assistants-have-a-study-or-learning-mode-and-how-do-i-turn-it-on)
- Complaints, Readwise: cost, no free tier, opaque after-trial billing, weak offline and search (secondary sources). — [Vendr](https://vendr.com/blog/do-you-use-readwise-app-how-much-are-you-paying-for-the-service); [aitoolsatlas](https://aitoolsatlas.ai/tools/readwise/pros-cons)
- Complaints, Goodreads and StoryGraph: dated design, notes and dates lost in migration (anecdotal). — [unstar.app](https://unstar.app/fr/blog/goodreads-storygraph-fable-hardcover-bookly-reading-tracker-apps-ranked-2026)
- Complaints, Anki and spaced repetition: missed days compound into a review backlog that feels impossible to clear, which leads to quitting. An Anki forum user proposed a "Backlog Recovery Planner" to prevent churn. This is anecdotal and feature-request evidence, not measured churn. — [Anki forums proposal](https://forums.ankiweb.net/t/proposal-backlog-recovery-planner-to-prevent-user-churn-burnout/70487); [mindomax](https://www.mindomax.com/flashcard-burnout); [neonlingo](https://www.neonlingo.com/blog/anki-burnout)
- Complaints, Notion trackers: criticised as glorified course lists with no active recall. — [DEV](https://dev.to/lux_seminare/how-to-build-learning-tracker-in-notion-3148)
- Complaints, Learn Your Way: a research prototype with a waitlist and only a small, short-horizon study. — [Google blog](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/education/learn-your-way/); [Maginative](https://www.maginative.com/article/learn-your-way-googles-new-ai-transforms-static-textbooks-into-interactive-personalized-lessons/)

### Inferences
- Review scheduling that is not scoped to a goal creates an unbounded backlog (Anki pattern). A goal-scoped, finite plan with a recovery or "reduce scope" path addresses a documented pain point.
- Reading-tracker progress is volume-based (books, pages, hours), which does not tell a learner whether they are advancing toward a stated goal.

### Gaps
- Direct Reddit and Hacker News threads on r/getdisciplined, r/learnprogramming, r/ObsidianMD, r/Anki were not retrieved. User complaints are from blogs, forums and aggregators, and are weaker than first-hand threads.
- Prerequisite, sequencing and progress details for Khan Academy mastery, Duolingo, Zotero, Mochi, Obsidian and Notion are unconfirmed (no official docs fetched).
- Complaint evidence for ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini learning modes (for example "forgets context between sessions" or "no persistent plan") was not found in a citable source.

## What features recur across the best tools, and which are rare?

### Takeaway
Resource curation, quizzes and flashcards, and simple progress or goal counters recur. Learner-defined goals, prerequisite graphs, time budgeting and a plan that adapts to missed time are rare. Based on what I verified, a goal-scoped plan joined to the learner's own reading is largely absent.

### Cited Findings
- Resource curation recurs in roadmap.sh (guides, videos, lesson packs), Metacademy (resource pointers per concept), LinkedIn paths (curated playlists) and Readwise (saves from many sources). — [roadmap.sh](https://roadmap.sh/); [hunch.net](https://hunch.net/?p=2714); [LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/learning/answer/a703777); [Readwise](https://readwise.io/pricing)
- Review scheduling and quizzing recur across Readwise, RemNote, Anki, Coursera Coach, NotebookLM and Gemini. — [Readwise](https://readwise.io/pricing); [RemNote blog](https://www.remnote.com/blog/best-anki-alternatives); [edScoop](https://edscoop.com/coursera-ai-powered-online-learning-coach/); [Chrome Unboxed](https://chromeunboxed.com/massive-notebooklm-update-adds-ai-powered-flashcards-quizzes-and-a-personal-tutor-mode/)
- Time estimates per concept appear in Metacademy. I did not find explicit time-budgeting against a learner's available hours in any tool reviewed. — [hunch.net](https://hunch.net/?p=2714)
- Goal framing in reading apps is quantitative (StoryGraph: books, pages or hours), not outcome-based. — [Reedsy comparison](https://Reedsy.com/discovery/blog/goodreads-vs-storygraph)
- Concept and prerequisite graphs appear in academic and Metacademy work. Research on automatically building concept prerequisite graphs exists (for example LectureBank), which signals feasibility but not mainstream product adoption. — [LectureBank paper](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1811.12181); [Sabanci University](https://research.sabanciuniv.edu/39248)
- Learn Your Way offers mind maps from source material, which suggests graph-like views are emerging in AI study tools. — [Google blog](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/education/learn-your-way/)

### Inferences
- Recurring: curated resources, flashcards and quizzes, and course-completion counters.
- Rare: user-stated goal as the root object; time budgeting; plan re-flow after missed time; prerequisite and concept graph over the learner's own material; plans that mix the user's own documents with external suggestions.
- This is a judgement from a limited sample, not an exhaustive feature audit.

### Gaps
- No systematic feature-by-tool matrix could be verified, because official docs were not fetched for most tools.
- I found no evidence on whether any tool re-schedules a plan after the learner falls behind (only the Anki forum request for a backlog planner).

## What is the evidence of why people abandon self-directed learning plans and reading lists?

### Takeaway
Time is the dominant stated cause, alongside perceived content value, course design, social context and unclear definitions of "completion". Practitioner accounts add backlog guilt and passive lists. There is little rigorous evidence specific to personal reading plans.

### Cited Findings
- A qualitative study of 34 MOOC learners (Eriksson, Adawi and Stöhr, 2017, Journal of Computing in Higher Education) found four dropout factors: perceived content, perceived course design, social situation and personal characteristics, and the ability to find and manage time. The title is "Time is the bottleneck". — [Chalmers research record](https://research.chalmers.se/en/publication/?id=249334)
- The study opens by noting that over 90% of MOOC learners never finish. A review article cites a completion rate under 10% (Alraimi et al., 2015). Both figures are old and MOOC-specific. — [Chalmers](https://research.chalmers.se/publication/?id=249334)
- A study of adult MOOC learners as self-directed learners found enrolment reasons ranged from personal enjoyment to professional development, and that learners define completion or success differently. — [Online Learning Journal](https://olj.onlinelearningconsortium.org/index.php/olj/article/view/889)
- A survey of 664 learners linked the "transactional distance" between learners and content to self-directed learning. — [ijlter.org](https://ijlter.org/index.php/ijlter/article/download/4392/pdf)
- Practitioner pieces say reading backlogs grow because discovery outpaces reading capacity, and that a plain read-later list is passive. Proposals include a small WIP limit, a finite queue, and resurfacing. These are opinion posts. — [engineered.at](https://engineered.at/articles/too-many-books); [DEV: read-later graveyard](https://dev.to/the_nortern_dev/your-read-later-list-is-a-graveyard-it-is-time-to-stop-hoarding-388g); [concepts.dsebastien.net](https://concepts.dsebastien.net/concept/reading-list/)
- A Reddit post describes self-hosting a backlog and scheduler app for learning material to avoid abandoning courses halfway. This is one anecdote. — [Reddit snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1tw4fxa/snapshots/2026-06-04T00%3A44%3A37.774434Z)
- Spaced-repetition backlog dynamic: missed days compound, skipped sessions grow the queue and then people quit. These are vendor and practitioner descriptions, not measured churn. — [mindomax](https://www.mindomax.com/flashcard-burnout); [studycardsai](https://studycardsai.com/blog/anki-burnout-solution); [Anki forums](https://forums.ankiweb.net/t/proposal-backlog-recovery-planner-to-prevent-user-churn-burnout/70487)
- Dissent: one commenter says people quit Anki simply because they dislike it, and a 12-year user quit with no regret. — [Japanese Level Up](https://japaneselevelup.com/?p=26463)

### Inferences
- For time-poor adults, "time is the bottleneck" supports designing around small time budgets, short sessions and explicit scope control.
- Disagreement over what counts as completion suggests a learner-defined goal and "done" criterion may reduce perceived failure. This is an inference, not a tested claim.
- Content from some sites in the first search (several near-identical ".it.com" explainer pages) looked low quality and was excluded.

### Gaps
- No rigorous data on abandonment of personal reading lists or learning plans (as distinct from MOOCs). I found no peer-reviewed study on it.
- No verified Hacker News thread. The search did not surface one, and Algolia HN search was not used.
- No independent survey of spaced-repetition app dropout. Retrieval-practice efficacy claims in vendor blogs (for example a 2023 cohort result, the Cepeda 2006 meta-analysis) were not checked against the original papers and are deliberately not reported as findings.
- Self-regulated learning literature (goal setting, implementation intentions) was not reviewed.

## Where is the whitespace for a goal-scoped, reading-centred curriculum tool?

### Takeaway
The verified gap is a single object, the learner's stated goal, that owns a living, finite reading plan built from material the learner chooses, with notes, tags, progress and a concept graph. Incumbents each cover one slice: roadmaps (structure), Readwise and Zotero (capture), Anki and RemNote (recall), and AI tutors (explanation). That is a hypothesis for the product team to test, not a proven market gap.

### Cited Findings
- Roadmap and path products start from the provider's catalogue, not from the learner's goal or attached material. — [roadmap.sh](https://roadmap.sh/); [LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/learning/answer/a703777)
- Learn Your Way, the closest to "your own material becomes a learning experience", is a waitlisted research prototype aimed at students, and it personalises presentation rather than sequence. — [Google blog](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/education/learn-your-way/)
- Gemini Guided Learning's launch post describes questions, quizzes and multimodal explanation, with no mention of learning plans. — [Google blog](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/education/guided-learning/)
- Users hand-assemble Zotero plus Notion or Obsidian queues, or self-host scheduler apps, to manage a learning backlog. This signals unmet demand for an integrated tool. — [Medium: PhD Tips](https://medium.com/@GenShanahan/phd-tips-the-project-and-knowledge-management-template-a7f67e94d9ae); [Reddit snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1tw4fxa/snapshots/2026-06-04T00%3A44%3A37.774434Z)
- Pricing anchors for a paid product: Readwise Full $9.99/mo, RemNote about $8/mo, Khanmigo $4/mo. — [Readwise](https://readwise.io/pricing); [RemNote blog](https://www.remnote.com/blog/best-anki-alternatives); [Khanmigo](https://khanmigo.ai/pricing)
- General-purpose AI tutors (ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude) are low-priced or free, so a new tool cannot rely on tutoring alone as its value. — [echai.ventures](https://echai.ventures/how-founders-use-ai/learning-with-ai/which-ai-assistants-have-a-study-or-learning-mode-and-how-do-i-turn-it-on)

### Inferences
- Candidate whitespace, each a hypothesis: (1) goal-first onboarding; (2) a finite, reading-centred plan with explicit time budget and WIP limit; (3) plan re-flow or "reduce scope" after missed time, which answers the backlog-spiral complaint; (4) a concept graph and notes drawn from the learner's own reading; (5) light, goal-scoped retrieval prompts instead of an unbounded flashcard queue; (6) data portability, because migration loss is a recurring complaint in book trackers.
- Likely competitive threats: Google (NotebookLM adds Learning Guide, flashcards, quizzes and saved progress, and Learn Your Way adds personalised material), plus Readwise adding plan features. NotebookLM is the nearest adjacent product to verify hands-on.
- Because tutors are free or cheap, differentiation should rest on persistence, ownership and structure rather than on explanations.

### Gaps
- No verified evidence of willingness to pay among time-poor adult self-improvers, and no market sizing.
- NotebookLM and ChatGPT projects or memory were not tested hands-on, so I cannot confirm whether either supports a persistent goal-scoped plan.
- A targeted check of Product Hunt, Reddit and HN for 2024-2026 consumer "AI learning roadmap" launches was not completed. A direct competitor may exist that I did not find.
